# Proof that $B_1$ is Not a $\sigma$-Algebra

## **Definitions**
Let $J$ be the collection of all intervals in $[0,1]$, including open, closed, and half-open intervals:

$$
J = \lbrace [a,b], (a,b], [a,b), (a,b) \mid 0 \leq a \leq b \leq 1 \rbrace.
$$

Define $B_1$ as the set of all **finite or countable unions of elements of $J$**:

$$
B_1 = \lbrace \bigcup_{i=1}^{n} I_i \mid I_i \in J, n \in \mathbb{N} \rbrace \cup \lbrace \bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty} I_i \mid I_i \in J \rbrace.
$$

Prove that $B_1$ is **not a $\sigma$-algebra**

---

## **Proof (by Contradiction)**

1. **Assume that $B_1$ is a $\sigma$-algebra.**
2. Let $K$ be the **Cantor set**.
3. Since $K^c$ (the complement of the Cantor set) is in $B_1$, closure under complements implies that $K \in B_1$.
4. By definition of $B_1$, if $K \in B_1$, then $K$ must be expressible as a **finite or countable union of elements of $J$**:

$$ 
   K = \bigcup_{i} I_i, \quad I_i \in J.
$$

5. Each $I_i$ is an interval of the form $[a,b], (a,b], [a,b), (a,b)$ where either:
   - $a = b$ (a **singleton interval**),
   - or $a \neq b$ (a **proper interval**).

### **Case 1: All $I_i$ are singleton intervals**  
   - If every $I_i = [k, k]$ is a **singleton containing only Cantor numbers**, then we have an **infinite countable union of singletons**.
   - But $K$ is **uncountable**, so a countable union of singletons **cannot cover all of $K$**.  

### **Case 2: At least one $I_i$ is a proper interval $[a, b]$ with $a < b$**  
   - In this case, the interval $[a, b]$ **must contain at least one non-Cantor number**.
   - To show this, consider the **ternary representations** of $a$ and $b$:
     $a = 0.a_1 a_2 a_3 \dots_3, \quad b = 0.b_1 b_2 b_3 \dots_3$ 
   - Let $j$ be the **first index where $a_j \neq b_j$**:
     $j = \min \lbrace k \mid a_k \neq b_k \rbrace$
   - Construct the number:
     $x = 0.a_1 a_2 \dots a_{j-1} 1 0 0 0 \dots_3$
   - Since $x$ contains the digit "1" in its ternary expansion, it **cannot be in $K$** (as Cantor numbers only contain 0s and 2s in base 3).
   - Thus, $I_i$ contains at least one non-Cantor number, contradicting our assumption that $K$ is a **countable union of intervals from $J$**.

$$
\Rightarrow B_1 \text{ is NOT a } \sigma \text{-algebra}.
$$
